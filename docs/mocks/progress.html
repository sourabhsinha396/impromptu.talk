Progress, drawn for somebody trying to get better

Only the bottom of the streak page changes. The tiles, the calendar and Recent stay as built and are faded here for their place. The question the block answers is the learner's: is this working, what should I work on next, and was today a relapse or a bad day. Every number below is arithmetic over what a round already stores; nothing is fetched or written that was not already there.

Top to bottom

  • Then and now. Your first five rounds against your last five, one row per skill: speaking (seconds of the minute with a voice in them), time to start, restarts, filling the time, landing an ending on a full stop, your worst habit word, different words. A hollow dot is then, a filled dot is now, the shaded stretch is comfortable where one exists, and each row is one sentence in plain words. Sentences, not lines, because the proof reads as a sentence: "you now speak for 51 seconds of your minute; it was 36". From six rounds (first three against last three) up to ten; under that it says how many more it needs.
  • One thing to work on. The skill furthest from comfortable over the last five rounds, named, with the round that shows it and one thing to do. A coach does this and a dashboard never does. Next week it is either fixed or still the one.
  • Your floor is rising. Your worst round this week against your first week's average, on the skill a beginner feels most (silence). Improvement in impromptu speaking is mostly the bad days getting less bad, and this sentence is true weeks before any average moves.
  • Three lines that matter, over the window: seconds of silence in the minute, restarts a round, time to start. Topic-independent, all falling with practice, each with the comfortable stretch shaded, a dot per round while there are twelve or fewer, and "was 4.6s · better" instead of "down from". Ums a minute is gone from here: a habit word is tracked in then-and-now as whichever word you lean on most.
  • By genre. Rounds carry their genre, so the page can say where you are weak and where you are fine: rounds, silence and time to start per genre, the hardest named. Under it, one line on prep: how you start with none against how you start with thirty seconds.
  • Firsts. The first minute with no holes, the first round with no restarts, the first clean ending, the first full minute, each dated and a link to that round; the ones not yet reached sit greyed with "not yet". Honest milestones, and the thing people screenshot.
  • Your first minute and your latest as waves, the round page's drawing, each with its date.

Free and Pro

  • Free keeps its five-day window, so the three lines and the two waves work for everybody. Then-and-now, the floor, genres and firsts need history to mean anything, and history is what the plan sells; free sees the headings with one sentence saying what Pro would show, as the calendar already does.

Reversed and left out

  • This widens the "three metrics and not nine" call from earlier today. Three lines stay three; the rest are sentences, dots and small tables, not more curves.
  • Left out: pace as a trend (topic-dependent, decided), any composite score (a number with nothing behind it), anything about the argument (that needs a model and belongs to the deferred paid critique).
  • Restarts and the habit word need one pass over each round's stored transcript and timings. Fine for the forty rounds the window draws; firsts over a year of Pro would want a small stored column later.
