Waiting for the report: the problem, and three ways out

The round ends, the three tiles land instantly, and then the screen sits still for anything from two seconds to fifteen while the audio uploads and comes back read. Today that wait is a grey hairline that pulses between full and half opacity. On white, --line is #e7e5e4, so the entire animation happens inside about one percent of contrast: nothing on the screen appears to move. It reads as finished and broken rather than as busy, and the words underneath say "Reading your round back..." with nothing on the page agreeing with them.

All three options below animate for real. Switch Waiting to When it lands to see what arrives, and whether the page jumps when it does. Both report drawings are here, because the wait happens on each of them.

1. The minute, already drawn

  • The wave is not a placeholder, it is the round. The browser measured the speech and the silence itself, fifty times a second, for the whole minute - that timeline is the thing it is uploading. So it already holds everything the wave is drawn from, and can draw it the instant the round ends. Only the words and the numbers are waiting on the server.
  • A read head crosses it, again and again. The wave sits dimmed, a line travels left to right, and the strokes brighten behind it. That is the copy, drawn: your minute is being read back. It is the only loading animation on the site that would mean something rather than merely move.
  • Nothing moves when the report lands. The wave is already at its final size and place; the sentence and the bands appear underneath it. Flip between the two states and it does not shift a pixel.
  • It removes something instead of adding something. There is no waiting component to build and keep: the report starts with the half of itself that needs no network.
  • Costs: the wave has to come from the browser's own timeline rather than the server's pauses, so the long-gap colour is decided twice (same threshold, same numbers, two places). And a round with a dead microphone draws a flat wave for a second before it says it heard nothing.

2. The report's own frame

  • The finished layout, empty. Every card and band at its real size, with a soft accent shimmer travelling through, staggered so the screen reads as one thing filling and not as eight things blinking.
  • The titles are real. "Your minute", "Pace", "Long gaps", "Words you lean on" are known before any request is made, so they are set in ink from the start and only the values are ghosts. A skeleton that says what it is about is a different thing from a grey box.
  • Strongest on the filming report, where ten empty cards genuinely fill a screen and promise a lot is coming.
  • Costs: the most familiar and the least ours. It says nothing about this round, and on the compact report it is eight grey rectangles under a headline that has already said Day 4.

3. One line that moves, and names the step

  • Keeps today's shape and fixes both faults. The bar travels along its track instead of pulsing, in the accent rather than in the hairline colour, so it is visible from across a room.
  • The step is named, and only where naming it is true. Sending the audio has a real percentage, because the upload reports one, and on a phone on mobile data it is the slow half. After that there is one request in flight and no honest way to know how far through it is, so the bar goes indeterminate and the words say "Reading it back". No invented three-step progress.
  • Slow is said out loud. After twelve seconds: "Still reading. Your streak is already saved." That sentence answers the only question anybody actually has, which is whether the round is lost.
  • Costs: it is a spinner. It fills the wait with a machine's status rather than with the person's own minute, and the page still jumps when the report arrives under it.

True of all three

  • The waiting block keeps role="status", so a screen reader is told once, in words, and the movement stays decorative.
  • Everything stops under prefers-reduced-motion: option 1 shows the finished wave, 2 shows still shapes, 3 shows a flat track. Turn the setting on and reload to check.
  • The two failure lines do not change: a round with no microphone behind it draws nothing at all, and a round nobody was heard in still says "We could not hear you."
The recommendation: option 1, with option 3's slow line under it. The wave is the only one of the three that is not a placeholder - it is the report arriving in two halves, drawn from numbers the browser is holding anyway, and it makes the wait shorter for the honest reason that half of the thing is already there. Option 3's twelve-second sentence costs one line and belongs under any of the three, since it is the only place the round is promised to be safe. Option 2 is the fallback if the browser's wave turns out to disagree with the server's pauses often enough to matter.
