Exam and profession genres: proposal, not yet decided

Nothing here is built. What it needs from the owner is one call: grouped (tabs 1 and 3) or flat (tab 4). Everything else follows.

Why this is the sanctioned growth move

  • SPEC.md §3 already settled the shape: "Anything naming an occasion (an interview, an exam, a club) is a context, and a context is a genre." So an exam is not a fifth style and not a new concept - it is a genre row, which the seeder and the picker already handle.
  • PRICING.md §2 already settled the price: every built-in genre is free forever, "including any exam or profession genre that ever exists, because those pages are the growth engine." So this needs no pricing decision and cannot be paywalled later.
  • SPEC.md §3 already anticipated the growth: "Anime and manga was the one contested call and is the first to split out again if genre pages earn their traffic." The mechanism for adding a genre back is written down.
  • The markets line up with the ones already priced. IELTS and PTE traffic is heavily India and the Philippines, which are two of the eight currencies in PRICING.md §4 and two of the deepest discounts on the table.

The one real tension, which tab 4 exists to show

  • Ten was a decision, not an accident: "A flat scroll list stops working somewhere around fifteen, which is exactly why ten needs no shelves: there are no category headers in the picker, no search box and no blurbs." Four more genres makes fourteen, which is inside that limit but spends all of it.
  • Tab 4 draws the flat fourteen so the cost is visible rather than argued. Two things go wrong there and neither is about length: "IELTS" and "Culture" are not peers, so a flat list asks somebody to choose between a subject and a situation; and the ten genres stop naming themselves the moment something in the list needs explaining.
  • Tabs 1 and 3 draw the grouped answer. The ten stay a flat unheaded list, exactly as decided, and the occasions sit in a second labelled group. This is not a new pattern: the picker already carries a labelled group for Yours (§8), so the shelf exists and this is its second tenant.
  • Recommended: grouped. It keeps the settled ten intact rather than reopening them, and the heading is doing honest work, since the two groups answer different questions.

What the exam page has to do that a genre page does not

  • Somebody arriving from a search engine has typed a question, so the page answers it above the topics. The one thing worth saying is that the tool's own defaults already are the test's format: a minute to prepare, up to two minutes to speak. That is not marketing, it is the settings in SPEC.md §4, and it is the strongest sentence available on the page.
  • Topics still group by the four styles, not by the exam's own parts. Grouping by part would need a second axis on Topic, and the four styles already carry every topic. Deliberately not proposed.
  • Every topic stays plain text on the page, which is the rule that makes the ten genre pages rank in the first place (§3).

What is unresolved, for the owner to call

  • The names, and the trademarks behind them. IELTS, PTE and Toastmasters are all trademarks of other organisations. Describing practice topics for a test is ordinarily fine; implying it is the official thing is not. So the page must never claim affiliation, and the drawn page carries one plain line at the foot saying so. Whether the fourth genre is called "Table topics" or "Speaking clubs" is the same question with a smaller answer, and this mock draws the safer of the two.
  • How many. Four are drawn. The floor is 40 topics a genre (§3), so four is 160 topics to write before any of it ships, and the bank goes from 1000 to 1160.
  • The icons. A genre carries an icon from a fixed set of 26 (§2). The four drawn here are proposals from that set, not new glyphs.
  • Whether the group has its own hub page. Drawn as a section of /genres, which keeps every genre one hop from every other. A separate /practise-for would be a second hub competing with the first for the same links.

IELTS speaking practice topics

Forty cue card topics to practise out loud, free and with no account. Read one, take a minute to think, then talk until the clock runs out.

The long turn gives you one minute to prepare and up to two minutes to speak. That is what this timer does by default, so practising here is practising at the real length.
