The Pro page: what changes from v0, and why

  • Cards first, then what they get you. The price is what somebody came to read; the list is what they read next, and it belongs to whichever plan is picked.
  • The list changes with the plan, and only where it truly does. Two of its six lines are the plan's streak and history length: a month on monthly and the pass, a year on annual and lifetime. The other four are the same on every plan, so they are said once. v0 printed the same seven lines inside both cards.
  • Picking a card picks a plan. One of the two is always on, and its segment says which of its two.
  • The pill is a segment, not a dropdown. Both options are worth seeing.
  • The pass is called "30 day pass", not "Monthly". v0 gave it the subscription's word on purpose, so both pills named a duration and the card above said whether it renewed. Two pills reading "Monthly" side by side is a riddle, and the card is not a big enough clue.
  • Currency is a dropdown of codes with flags, on the Plans row, because it changes every number below it. The code and not the country name: it is a third of the width and says the same thing, since what is being picked is what the price is written in. Five at a time, the one in use always among them.
  • The questions are an accordion (shadcn's shape: a hairline per row, the whole row is the trigger, one chevron that turns), with the first one open. v0 had three bullets in a list.
  • Much less prose. One short line under the headline. v0's page argued; this one prices.
  • A card nobody can buy from says which reason it is - already held, or not open yet. A card that goes quiet reads as broken.
  • The typos are gone. v0 shipped "Steak survives 2 missed days" and "Tacks 365 days of streak" on the live page.
  • Nothing here depends on the overlay. Every way it can fail ends at the ordinary checkout, which is what buys Pro.
  • Flags are images, not emoji, which is both what the no-emoji rule asks for and the only thing that renders on Windows. In the build they are eight small SVGs served from our own origin; here they come from a CDN.
