Your round, read back: what changes and why

The page is /streak/[id], the record of one round, reached on purpose from the streak page. The done screen on home stays exactly as built (bar, one sentence, the bands, the read-back behind a link), because it is a moment and the ten-second rule wants it short; it gains one underlined link, See the full report, into this page. Recharts is already a dependency of /streak and nowhere else, so every chart here is inside that decision. The mock draws them by hand so the file opens on its own.

What is new on the page, top to bottom

  • The minute as a wave. Strokes where there was a voice, a flat line where there was not, the holes you would notice in the warm colour, and nothing at all after the last word. The first version was a strip of green and red and took a key to read; a wave needs none, because everybody has seen one (owner's call, with a sketch). The heights are a texture and not loudness: no audio is kept and the report knows only when there was sound.
  • Fillers on the wave. Each um is a warm dot on the line at the second it was said, so "6 um" becomes "six ums, all of them in the first half". The transcriber's word clock places them; nothing new is stored.
  • Pace through the minute. Words a minute in each ten seconds, with the comfortable 130 to 170 band shaded. Run 30 shows what one number hides: 156, 120, 210, 78, 126, 75. A sprint at 0:20 and a fade from 0:40, and the single "235 wpm" said neither. The line under it is written by code from the peak and the trough, never by a model.
  • The shape of your round. The radar from the owner's screenshot, five axes: pace, start, gaps, sentences, fillers. The dashed pentagon is the comfortable range, the filled shape is today, the thin outline is your usual over the last twelve rounds (Pro). Beside it the same measures as bars in their real units, because "143 wpm" and "2.65s" are what a person understands and the radar is only the picture of them; on every bar the hollow ring is your usual. The caption says how many of the five are in range and which is furthest out. No average: an average of seconds and words a minute is a number with nothing behind it.
  • Words you leaned on, as a donut. ReUI's donut with a centre total. The ring is every word you said, the warm slices are fillers, the accent slices the words you lean on, the quiet remainder is the rest of your minute; the centre reads "18 of 129 words", and hovering a slice makes the centre answer with that word's count. The list beside it carries every count, never just the top three. The "ums a minute" band is gone: a cluster you can see needs no rate beside it (owner's call). Then said more than once: phrases of two or more words that came back ("most of the time" three times), and restarts, a two-word run begun twice within four seconds ("and that I used, and that I used"), which is the sound of losing the sentence and is the thing nobody notices in themselves.
  • Sentences. One thin column per sentence, its word count as the height, anything past 35 words in warm. Run 30 has one sentence of 56 words strung on "and". Impromptu speech fails this way more than any other and no count of pauses shows it.
  • Three tiles for the facts that are not a scale: when you started, when you finished and whether on a full stop, how many different words. A three-column grid, never two and one.
  • Read it back, open. On this page it is the record, so it is not behind a link. Pauses inline where they fell, fillers and leaned-on words marked as today, restarts dotted underneath.

Taken from ReUI's chart set, and left there

  • Taken. The donut with a centre total and its active-segment variant (the leaned-on words). The radar with a filled shape (the shape of your round), with a dashed target polygon added because a radar with no reference shape is a blob. The card heading pattern, a title with one number at the right, which every section here already wears. The plain bar chart, which the sentence columns are.
  • Left. Glowing markers and entrance animation (decoration; Motion is not installed, by decision, and the site respects reduced motion). Stripe and crosshatch fills (they exist for colour-blind and print readers as an opt-in, not as a look). The Lighthouse radial of concentric arcs (three arcs read worse than three bars, and the values end up printed sideways). Forecast zones (nothing here is a forecast). 3D isometric bars.
  • In the build every one of these is Recharts, which /streak already loads: PieChart for the donut, RadarChart for the shape, AreaChart for pace, plain divs for the bars and the minute.

Where each number comes from

  • Stored today, never shown: word timings (words_at) for every Pro round, the pause list, speaking_ratio, trail_off, the full transcript. Everything in the filler dots, the pace curve, the repeats, the restarts and the sentence columns is arithmetic over these. No schema change, no new call.
  • One request parameter away, free tier: Groq's transcription accepts response_format=verbose_json with timestamp_granularities[]=word on whisper-large-v3-turbo at the same price. That gives Free the pace curve and the pause-in-sentence read-back too; only the ums stay Pro, because Whisper deletes them. The Free tab is drawn with that change made; today Free has the transcript and nothing timed.
  • Dropped on the floor today, worth keeping: AssemblyAI's per-word confidence and audio_duration. The duration is a second clock and would have caught run 30's stretched timeline (see below). Confidence is not drawn here on purpose: low confidence on an accent is the transcriber's problem, not the speaker's, and marking it as "unclear" would be unfair before it was useful.
  • Later, browser side, both tiers: the loudness envelope is sampled fifty times a second for the pause map and then thrown away. Keeping 240 numbers a minute (four a second) would draw volume over the minute and make trail-off a shape rather than a ratio. It is not audio and stores nothing anybody said. Pitch is possible the same way and is left out: a laptop microphone's pitch track is noisy and a confidence score is refused already.
  • Available from the transcriber and rejected: sentiment per sentence (tone is not what this exercise trains), key phrases (a lexical guess at "focus"), entities, topic categories, summaries and LeMUR prose (the report writes no prose, by decision). A language flag is worth turning on when the filler list goes multilingual, not before.

Two things the data said while drawing this

  • Run 30's browser clock ran four times fast. Its stored timeline ends at 226 seconds for a 58 second round; the transcriber's words end at 54.7. Every respin on the topic screen starts another level ticker without stopping the last, so four spins meant four tickers feeding one array. The built page's "0:07 to start", "4 long gaps" and "5.02s" are artefacts of that; the transcriber's clock says 0:02, five gaps over 1.5s and 2.65s. This mock draws run 30 from the word clock. The fix is a few lines in voice.ts and is filed separately.
  • Pace is measured one way and judged another. analysis.words divides by speaking time with the silences removed, which is articulation rate and sits around 200 to 240 for ordinary speech; the band it is judged against, 130 to 170, is the wall-clock norm. So nearly every round will read "Rushed". Run 30 is 231 over speaking time and 143 over the clock. The mock uses the clock for the curve and the band, and proposes the same for the number.
