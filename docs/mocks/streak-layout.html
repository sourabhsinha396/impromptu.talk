The streak page empties out as the streak fills up

The calendar and the Recent list share a row on a laptop. The comment above TwoColumns says why: "the calendar is the wide thing and the list is the tall one, so they sit side by side and finish together instead of leaving a column of nothing down the right of a long page." The premise is wrong. A thirty-day calendar is five columns of seven squares - about 180 pixels tall, and it never grows. The list grows by one row per round. So the two never finish together, and the gap between them is the streak: the better somebody practises, the emptier their page looks.

It is not bounded, either. Free lists the last 25 rounds and Pro lists up to a thousand (FREE_RUNS, PRO_RUNS in streaks.py), so a Pro speaker a year in has a column of nothing several screens tall. Switch to 60 rounds above to see where that is going.

The same pairing is wrong at every window. Five days is a strip of named cards beside a 25-row list. Thirty days is the block in the screenshot. A year is the only window that already escapes it, because isWide gives the year the whole width and puts the list underneath - which is the answer, arriving 91 days too late.

1. One column

  • The calendar always takes the whole width, and the list sits under it. Which is what the year already does, so this is the year's rule applied to every window instead of only the longest one.
  • At the whole width a month is one row of thirty squares, not a five by seven block. That is a better calendar as well as a wider one: a row reads left to right like time does, and the gap where you missed three days is a gap, not a hole in a grid whose columns are weeks nobody counts.
  • The list, given the width, runs in two columns and halves its height. Nineteen rounds is ten and nine, and it still reads top to bottom then across.
  • It removes something: TwoColumns and isWide both go, and the page stops having two layouts that depend on a plan.

2. The calendar goes up with the tiles

  • The calendar is a small object, so it belongs with the other small objects. Streak, topics, minutes and the month sit in one band at the top, and the list has the page to itself underneath.
  • The top of the page becomes the whole answer to "how am I doing", which is what somebody clicking the flame came for, and everything below it is the record.
  • Costs: four things in a row is a dashboard, and the three tiles were deliberately three. The month has to shrink to fit beside them, which is the opposite of what option 1 does with it.

3. A shorter list

  • Keep the two columns and stop the list growing: show the last eight, then "See all 19 rounds". The two columns roughly balance, and the page is the same height at round 19 as at round 900.
  • The thousand-row cap needs this whatever else happens. A list nobody scrolls to the end of is not a record, it is a wall, and the page has no other way to get shorter.
  • Costs: it is the least of the three. The columns only balance at one length, so the void comes back the moment somebody has a slow week and the calendar is emptier than the list is long; and it hides rounds behind a click on the one page whose job is to show them.
The recommendation: 1, and take 3's cap with it. One column fixes the shape - nothing on this page has a partner it can be left waiting for - and the month at full width is a better drawing of a month than the block it replaces. The cap is a separate fault the layout was hiding: a Pro speaker's thousand rows are a wall at any width, so the list shows the last ten or so and says how many there are. Option 2 is the one to take if the top of the page should answer everything and the rest should be the archive.
