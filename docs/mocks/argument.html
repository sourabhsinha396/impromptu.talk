The case you made: what changes and why

Everything on the round page today says how somebody spoke. Nothing on it can say whether they did what the topic asked. Run 30 is the case: "Argue for owning one good pen" got a story about a pen fight, the page reported 143 words a minute and one um, and the one thing a learner needed to hear was not on it. SPEC.md §14 deferred exactly this ("model-based critique of the argument, paid, via OpenRouter, fed the deterministic report"); on the owner's call today it moves to the front, Pro only, and Free sees a blurred sample of the same components. Two frames below: the round page with its new first section, then the bottom of the streak page with its new row.

What the model does, and what it never does

  • It is asked for structure, not prose. It gets the topic, the sentences as the report already splits them, and the delivery facts (time to start, pace, the longest gap, whether the round ended on a full stop). It answers with a role for every sentence, one of six words; whether the topic was answered, one of three (yes, half, no); one sentence of verdict; one sentence to do next time. That is the whole answer, and it is stored once on the report row when the report attaches. Read a round in a year and it says the same thing, because nothing is asked twice.
  • Never a score, never a rewrite, never a paragraph. The three logged refusals hold. The verdict word is code from the category, the same word for the same category every time; only the two sentences are the model's, and each is capped at twenty-two words.
  • Everything drawn is arithmetic over the roles and what is already stored. The sentences already have word timings, so a role per sentence puts each role on the minute at its second. The point's clock, "answered 3 of your last 10", the ten squares and the time-to-your-point trend are all code over stored rows. The model draws nothing.
  • Fed the delivery report so it cannot contradict it. The next-time line on the good round says start the close ten seconds earlier, because the model was told the bell cut the last sentence. Without that it would have praised the ending.

The section, top to bottom

  • First on the page, above pace. The topic asked for something; this is whether it was given. How the minute sounded comes after what it said.
  • The verdict. A word in the accent when the topic was answered, in ink when half, in the warm colour when missed; then the model's one sentence. "Half answered" is a real state, and the commonest: the point made in the first breath and then abandoned for the story, which is what run 30 did.
  • The sentences on the minute. Every sentence is a block from its first word to its last, coloured by what it was doing. The point in the strong accent, a reason in the accent, an example paler, an aside grey, a wind-up hollow and dashed (talk that has not started yet), the close in ink. Run 30 reads at a glance: one short strong block at 0:02, then forty-eight seconds of pale example, and nothing in ink at the end. Hover a block for the sentence.
  • The point, flagged at its second. "Your point, 0:02" stands over the block that made it. When no sentence made one, "no point made" sits at the right end in warm, which is the whole finding for the third tab.
  • A legend of only the roles this round used. Six squares under every round would be a lesson; three under this one is a key.
  • Next time. One sentence, plain, about the case and not the delivery, since the bands already carry delivery advice behind "What to do". This one is not behind a disclosure: it is the reason the section exists.

The six roles

  • The point: the sentence that does what the topic asked. The claim for an argument, the thing itself for an explanation, the premise for a story.
  • A reason: why the point holds. An example: a story or a case that shows it. Both are support; the colours say which kind, because a minute of examples with no reason and a minute of reasons with no example are different habits.
  • Wind-up: restating the topic, saying that it matters, clearing the throat in words. The stall band catches silence before the first word; this catches the ten seconds of talk before the first thought, which the bands cannot see.
  • An aside: off the point. The close: the sentence that lands it again or wraps up.

On the streak page

  • Two cards under the three trend cards, one row, so a phone stacks them and a laptop does not wrap two and one. Answered the topic: ten squares for the last ten rounds, oldest to latest, filled when answered, half filled when half, a warm tint when missed; the number is how many were answered. Time to your point: the same card as time to start, seconds on the clock until the point was made, lower is better, the comfortable stretch under 12 seconds shaded. Rounds with no point have no time and are left off this line; the squares beside it already show them.
  • Both are the "am I improving" question about the case, which is the question the progress page is for. The three cards today are all about delivery.

Free

  • The same section and the same row, from one fixed sample round, blurred, with one line. The sample is the promotion story on the second tab, the same round for everybody, and its header says "a sample". Not their own round blurred: the model never ran on a free round, so there is nothing of theirs to blur, and a blur over something unseen reads as a bug rather than a door. The line is the streak page's own pitch pattern (a sentence and a link into /pro): "Pro reads what you said."
  • Free's own round page keeps everything it has today, unblurred, above and below the sample.

Where it runs, and what it costs

  • Once, when the report attaches, for a Pro round that got a transcript. The done screen already fills the report in when it arrives; this adds a model call to that wait and nothing to the run POST. A refusal, an empty answer, an answer in the wrong shape or fifteen seconds of silence leaves the report exactly as it is, and the page shows no section rather than an apology.
  • No new allowance. The call needs a transcript, transcripts are already metered in minutes, and a Pro month is two hours of them; at the model's rate that ceiling is a few cents. The same client and the same model as topic generation (apps/topics/openrouter.py, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite via OpenRouter), moved to apps/common/ now that two apps call it.
  • Older Pro rounds from before this show no section. Nothing is read after the fact.
  • Not on the done screen. It stays a moment, and "See the full report" is already there. If you want the verdict line under the done screen's headline, say so; it is a line, not a card.
  • The privacy page gains one sentence: a Pro transcript reaches a model. The transcript already reaches a transcriber, and the page says that.

To approve or change

  • The section's place (first). The word "point" over "claim" (it has to fit a story and an explanation too). The six roles and their colours. "Answered it", "Half answered", "Missed it". The two cards. The sample and its one line.
