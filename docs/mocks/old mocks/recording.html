Say it during the round, not after it

What happens now. The microphone is only ever reported on once the round is over, and only in one of the two ways it can fail. A microphone that opened and heard nothing gives heard: false, and the done screen says "We could not hear you. Check your microphone." after the whole minute has been spoken. A microphone that never opened at all - refused, no device, a browser without MediaRecorder - writes report: "off" and the done screen draws nothing, silently, on that round and on every round after it. For the whole minute a working round and a broken one look exactly the same, so there is no moment at which somebody could have fixed it.

The three states, and where each one is said

  • Heard. A level meter under the clock, moving with your voice. Five bars on the centre line the report's wave is already drawn on, so it is the same vocabulary, live: fat strokes instead of thin ones, and remembering nothing. No label and no icon, because a meter that is moving is already the sentence. Owner's call, over the two alternatives in tab 4.
  • Open but silent (muted hardware, the wrong input picked, a headset that never connected). Caught on the topic screen, before the clock, so nothing is lost. The level ticker is already running there, and a live microphone in a silent room is never digital silence - it always has a floor. Exact zero for two seconds is a dead input, not a quiet room.
  • Heard nothing once you began (working, but pointed away or with the input volume at nought). Caught four seconds into the speaking, when the meter has not moved. The bars go warm and flat, one sentence appears, and the clock keeps running. Nothing in the speaking loop may depend on the microphone working: the round still happens and the streak still counts.

What this costs

  • Nothing new is measured. The level is already sampled fifty times a second for the timeline, and this only draws it.
  • The meter remembers nothing, so nobody watches their own pauses pile up while they are still speaking. That was the argument against drawing the live wave, and it is what the meter buys.
  • The check that runs before the clock says nothing when it passes. Silence is the good state, so the topic screen keeps its near-zero words and the ten-second rule survives. Tab 3 has a button for the other reading of that, where the meter appears on the topic screen too and lets somebody confirm the microphone before they commit the minute.

Three ways to say "we can hear you"

All three run off the same live level. The only question is how much of the round each one remembers.

Chosen

A level meter

Remembers a moment

The one control everybody has already seen, so nothing about it has to be explained, and it is not as borrowed as it looks: five fat strokes on the centre line the report's wave is drawn on. It shows the present moment and no history, so it proves the microphone is live without putting your pauses in front of you while you are still speaking.

The minute, drawing itself

Remembers the whole round

The report's own wave, live, so the picture you watched being made is the picture you are handed at the end. Not taken: it shows your pauses while you are still in them, which is the one thing on this screen that could change the minute being measured, and it puts a second clock beside the clock.

A pulse

Remembers nothing

The quietest of the three, and the only one carrying an icon. Not taken: one blob breathing says something is on, but not that it is hearing you, and a microphone glyph in the middle of the round names the machinery on a screen that is meant to be a person and a clock.
