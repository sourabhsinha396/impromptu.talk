The whole minute as a cloud

Every word kept - pronouns, conjunctions, articles, all of it - sized by how often it was said, packed across and down, and shaded so the most used are the strongest. Drawn from a real round, "Why cats are plotting something" at /streak/124, 111 words, so every size and every shade below is a count and not a sketch.

How it is drawn

  • Size is the count, on a square-root scale between 13 and 62 pixels. Linear sizing makes one word fill the box and the rest a hedge; the square root is what every word cloud since the first one has used, and it is why "they" at eight is large without being the only thing you can see.
  • Shade is the count too, in five steps down one accent ramp: the most used is `--accent-strong`, then `--accent`, then three mixes toward `--muted`. One hue, never a second colour, so the picture still belongs to whoever picked their accent - try Coral and Cyan above. Saying it twice, in size and in shade, is the point: at a glance the deep words are the big words, and the picture has a middle and edges instead of being a uniform field.
  • Across and down, nothing in between. Every word is either horizontal or turned a quarter, which is the only rotation a reader can take without tilting their head. Words at forty-five degrees are the reason most word clouds are unreadable. The All across switch shows what is lost: a taller, looser block that reads more easily and looks like far less.
  • Packed on a spiral from the middle out, biggest first, each word tried against the ones already placed until it fits. The seed is fixed, so the same round draws the same cloud every time it is opened - Repack reseeds it to show what a different packing looks like, which is a thing the real page must never do.
  • Nothing needs the backend. The transcript is already on the round, so the browser counts its own words, the same way it already draws its own wave.

What the cloud is honest about, and what it is not

  • It answers a question nothing else on the page does: what did this minute sound like. On this round the biggest word is they, said eight times, against cats said once - a minute about cats in which the speaker said cats one time. The section as it stands cannot say that, because `leaned_on` is a crutch word list and drops pronouns before the page sees them.
  • Position means nothing. A word near the middle is only there because it was placed early, which is because it was said often, which the size already says. Nobody should read the arrangement, and the notes under the picture do not invite them to.
  • A minute is a small sample. Around 60 words are different and most are said once, so the whole bottom of the ramp is ties. That is exactly what the faintest shade means and why it is one step and not twenty.
  • It is a picture, not a reading. Which is why the sized line is still in the mockbar: the same counts, biggest first, on one line, with nothing packed. That one survives a phone and a screen reader. The cloud is the thing people screenshot.
The recommendation: the cloud on the round's own page, the sized line under it or in its place on a phone. The round page is where somebody goes to look at one minute properly, and a cloud is the only drawing here that shows the shape of a whole minute's vocabulary at once. Keep the size and the shade saying the same thing, keep the rotation to a quarter turn, and keep the seed fixed. The line is what a narrow screen and a screen reader get, and it loses nothing but the packing, which never carried anything.
