Done

The report, sized for filming

One round drawn three ways. The round: a minute on "Meetings should be optional." with a six second gap at 0:34, two seconds to start, 143 words a minute, four ums, two restarts and one run-on sentence. Every number below is that round, so the only thing changing between tabs is the drawing.

Why this exists. People film this. They hold a laptop up to a phone, or sit a phone on a stand pointed at the screen, and the reel is a vertical crop of the middle of that screen. Today's report is drawn for somebody reading a laptop at arm's length: a 40 pixel wave, 12.5 pixel numbers, four bands in two columns, sitting under the buttons at the bottom of the screen with nothing to spare, and the read-back and the link to the full report already off the bottom. Turn on What the phone sees and As filmed together over the Today tab: the crop takes about a third of the screen width, and what is inside it is a grey smear.

What changes

  • Every measure is a card. A box with a title, one big number and its comfortable stretch under it. A box is what survives a camera: an edge and a fill hold at any distance, where a row of text on a white field does not.
  • The whole reading, not the summary. The done screen's four bands become ten cards: the minute, whether you answered the topic, the shape against your usual, the four measures, the words you lean on, restarts, sentences. All of it is arithmetic over what is already stored for the round, the same numbers the round's own page draws, so nothing new is measured and no second call is made.
  • Nothing thinner than four pixels. A camera loses a hairline and a compressor eats what is left. The wave's strokes go from 1.5 to 4, the gap from 3 to 10, the track from 6 to 13, the marker from 12 to 24, and a card is a filled box with a 1.5 pixel edge rather than a line on white.
  • Colour carries the verdict. A measure outside its stretch is warm in the number as well as on the track, and the answered card is the accent. Muted grey holds only what nobody needs to read from across the room.
  • The read-back stays a link. A paragraph of transcript cannot be read on camera at any size. It is there for the person, not for the shot.
  • One screen, no scrolling on a laptop. The frame here is a laptop screen and it clips: anything below the fold does not exist to somebody holding the machine with both hands.

The board (a laptop, and what gets filmed)

  • Ten cards in a four column grid, sized so the last row ends at the bottom edge. Two cards are double width because their content is wide: the minute and the words you lean on.
  • The order is what a viewer needs in that order: the minute, whether the minute answered the topic, the shape, the four measures, the words, the sentences. Next time is one line at the foot, beside Spin again.
  • It does not fit a 9:16 crop and is not meant to: somebody filming a laptop head-on frames the screen, and this is the drawing for that shot and for a screen recording.

The column (the crop, and a phone)

  • The same cards in one column at 322 pixels, the width of the 9:16 crop of this screen. Nothing is redrawn: the grid goes to one column exactly as it does on a phone, so this is one design and not two.
  • It runs past the fold and scrolls, which a reel does anyway: the wave and the verdict are the first screen, the measures the second.

The setting

  • Named Report sized for filming, on the Additional settings page of the round's sheet. The site already says "sized for a phone filming a laptop" about the topic, so this is the same sentence about the report.
  • Not "big fonts": it is not a zoom. The layout, the order, the stroke weights and how much of the reading is shown all change.
  • Off by default, and it changes only the report. The round itself is already sized for filming.
Building this: the board on a laptop, the same cards in one column on a phone or inside the crop, behind the Additional settings switch. A card whose numbers a round does not have is not drawn, so a free round shows the measures and no verdict rather than a box saying nothing.
